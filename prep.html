Cape Town Climate Guide

Considering the Weather for Your Journey

When travelling in and around Cape Town, depending on the time and season you are visiting, you may need to consider the weather. The Cape Peninsula is famous for its distinct microclimates—where clear ocean skies on the Atlantic can meet mountain mists over the Twelve Apostles. Understanding the four seasons ensures you are perfectly equipped for an extraordinary expedition.

September – November

Spring

Daytime 17°C – 21°C 63°F – 70°F
Nighttime 9°C – 12°C 48°F – 54°F

The weather warms up progressively; the entire region experiences blooming wildflowers, vibrant King Proteas, and crisp, clear days. Ideal for West Coast San heritage journeys and coastal hikes.

Pack light breathable layers and a wind shell for breezy afternoon coastal trails.
December – February

Summer

Daytime 24°C – 28°C 75°F – 82°F
Nighttime 13°C – 18°C 55°F – 64°F

The warmest and driest time of year with long, sun-drenched days. Peak summer months are glorious for the Cape Peninsula, Boulders Beach penguins, and sunset viewpoints along Chapman's Peak.

Early morning ascents on Table Mountain (06:30–07:30) are recommended before midday heat. Bring 2L water.
March – May

Autumn

Daytime 20°C – 25°C 68°F – 77°F
Nighttime 11°C – 15°C 52°F – 59°F

Temperatures cool gently from summer peaks; wind conditions settle, leaving calm, golden light and extraordinarily clear mountain visibility. Widely regarded by local mountaineers as the finest hiking season.

Perfection for full-day traverses, Winelands cellar visits, and panoramic summit photography.
June – August

Winter

Daytime 12°C – 17°C 54°F – 63°F
Nighttime 7°C – 9°C 45°F – 48°F

The coldest period; mild, wet, and windy spells intersperse with brilliantly crisp, blue-sky winter days. Table Mountain's gorges run with fresh waterfalls, and valleys turn a lush, deep green.

A quality waterproof jacket, warm fleece, and boots with deep tread are essential for wet sandstone steps.
Gear Checklist

Universal Essential Packing List

Everything you need for comfort, safety, and confidence on Cape Town's mountain and cultural trails.

Footwear & Trail Grip

  • Broken-In Trail Shoes or Hiking Boots Crucial: Sturdy soles with deep rubber traction for sandstone scrambles. Avoid smooth sneakers.
  • Moisture-Wicking Hiking Socks Merino wool or technical synthetics prevent friction blisters during multi-hour descents.
  • Trekking Poles (Optional) Helpful for knees on steep descent routes like Platteklip Gorge or India Venster.

Smart Layering System

  • Breathable Technical Base Layer Quick-drying polyester or merino wool keeps sweat away from your skin as elevation increases.
  • Insulating Mid-Layer Fleece Table Mountain summit temperatures can drop 6°C to 10°C cooler than the city bowl below.
  • Wind & Waterproof Shell Jacket A lightweight jacket shields against sudden sea winds, tablecloth mists, and passing squalls.

Sun & Elemental Care

  • Wide-Brim Hat or Secure Peak Cap Protects face, neck, and ears during exposed ridge traverses without tree cover.
  • UV400 Polarized Sunglasses High UV levels in Southern Africa require dedicated optical eye protection.
  • Broad-Spectrum SPF 50+ Sunscreen & Lip Balm Reapply frequently, especially when ocean breezes mask the radiant heat of the sun.

Hydration & Daypack

  • Minimum 1.5L to 2L Water per Person There are no potable water points on the mountain trails. Hydration is non-negotiable.
  • Comfortable 15L – 25L Daypack Fitted with secure shoulder and chest straps to carry water, camera, and spare layers comfortably.
  • Personal Medication & Blister Care Keep personal inhalers, allergy medications, and blister plasters accessible in your bag.
Lead Guide Wisdom

Mountain Safety from Ian Naidoo

Table Mountain is not a city park—it is a rugged 1,000-meter wilderness rising directly out of the Southern Atlantic. Over 20+ years of leading expeditions, our safety record is rooted in respect for the mountain.

01

Watch the "Tablecloth" Cloud

When the south-easterly wind blows, thick cloud rolls over the flat summit plateau within minutes, dropping temperatures drastically and cutting visibility. We monitor real-time weather stations before and during every ascent.

02

Never Hike Unprepared or Alone

Paths like India Venster feature steep rocky ledges and scrambling sections. Our certified guiding team carries satellite communication, wilderness first-aid kits, and deep route knowledge to keep you safe at all times.

03

Respect the Living Flora

The Cape Floral Kingdom hosts over 9,000 plant species, 70% of which exist nowhere else on Earth. We stay strictly on designated paths to preserve ancient fynbos and prevent trail erosion.

04

Pack It In, Pack It Out

We practice strict Leave No Trace ethics. Every scrap of fruit peel, plastic, or waste must return down the mountain with us. We leave the landscape as pristine as when we arrived.
